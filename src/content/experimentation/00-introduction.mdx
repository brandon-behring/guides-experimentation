# Introduction

This guide is for mid-to-senior data scientists and ML engineers who already know what an A/B test is and want a single, equation-fluent reference that:

- gets you from "what is a p-value, again?" to "I can debug a SRM in a two-sided marketplace under interference" without a textbook detour,
- shows the math when it carries explanatory weight and skips it when it does not,
- treats variance reduction (CUPED), sequential testing, and heterogeneous treatment effects as first-class topics rather than appendices, and
- assumes you can read Python with pandas and statsmodels without hand-holding.

## Why randomization

<KeyIdea>
**Randomization breaks the back of confounding.** When treatment assignment is independent of every potential outcome (observed or unobserved), the difference in observed means is an unbiased estimator of the average treatment effect — without needing to model the world.
</KeyIdea>

The full equation-fluent derivation lands in Chapter 3 of the Phase 1 deliverable. For now: the conditional independence of treatment and potential outcomes is what makes the observed-mean-difference equal the true ATE. The math is in the Phase 1 chapter; the consequence is that running an experiment is the cleanest causal-inference move available when you can.

## SUTVA — the assumption that breaks first

The Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption has two parts:

1. **No interference.** Unit i's outcome does not depend on unit j's treatment assignment. This breaks in two-sided marketplaces (drivers in Lyft's experiment affect ride availability for control riders), in social networks (a treated user changes content for untreated friends), and in any system with shared global resources (caches, recommenders, queues).
2. **No hidden treatment variation.** "Treatment" is a single, well-defined intervention — not a mixture of bugged plus working code shipped to different cohorts.

Most of this guide assumes SUTVA holds. The exceptions are explicit: interference is covered in Chapter 12, ramp interaction in Chapter 13.

## The four-step loop

| Step | Output | Covered in |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothesize | A directional, falsifiable claim about a single metric | Ch 2 |
| Design | Sample size, MDE, allocation, guardrails, exclusions | Ch 4 to 6 |
| Run | The experiment, with SRM checks and monitoring | Ch 7 |
| Analyze | Point estimate, CI, significance, decision | Ch 8 to 11 |

Variance reduction (CUPED, regression adjustment) folds into the design and analyze steps. Sequential testing changes the analyze step. Heterogeneous treatment effects extend the analyze step.
